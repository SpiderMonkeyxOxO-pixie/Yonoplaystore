# Rummy Rules: How to Play 13-Card Indian Rummy, Step by Step

## Direct answer

In 13-card Indian rummy, each player is dealt 13 cards and tries to arrange all of them into valid groups: **sequences** (three or more consecutive cards of the same suit) and **sets** (three or four cards of the same rank in different suits). To declare, you need at least **two sequences, and one of them must be a pure sequence**, meaning it contains no joker. On each turn you draw one card and discard one. The first player to make a valid declaration wins the hand, and the others score penalty points for their unarranged cards.

## What you need to play

Indian rummy is usually played by two to six players with two standard 52-card decks plus printed jokers. Each player receives 13 cards. The remaining cards form the **closed deck**, placed face down, and one card is turned face up to start the **open deck** (the discard pile).

At the start of the game, one card is also picked at random as the **wild joker**. Every card of that rank, in any suit, acts as a joker for the rest of the hand. Printed jokers are jokers too.

## Sequences, the pure sequence and sets

These are the only groups that count. Worked examples follow in the list below.

- **Pure sequence:** three or more consecutive cards of one suit with no joker, for example 5♥ 6♥ 7♥, or 10♠ J♠ Q♠ K♠.
- **Impure sequence:** three or more consecutive cards of one suit where a joker replaces a missing card, for example 4♦ 🃏 6♦, where the joker stands in for 5♦.
- **Set:** three or four cards of the same rank in different suits, for example 9♣ 9♥ 9♠. A joker can complete a set, such as 9♣ 9♥ 🃏. Two cards of the same suit, such as 9♣ 9♣, do not make a set.
- **Aces:** an Ace can be low (A-2-3) or high (Q-K-A) in a sequence, but not both at once, so K-A-2 is not valid.

## How a turn works

Play moves around the table. Watch what other players pick from and discard to the open deck, because it hints at which cards they are collecting. On your turn:

1. **Draw one card**, either the top card of the closed deck or the top card of the open deck.
2. **Rearrange** your hand to build sequences and sets.
3. **Discard one card** face up onto the open deck, so you always hold 13 cards after your turn.

## How to declare and win

When all 13 of your cards form valid groups, you place your final card on the "finish" slot and **declare** your hand. A typical winning hand might be a pure sequence of four cards, an impure sequence of three, and two sets of three. If you declare and your hand does not meet the rules below, it is a **wrong declaration**, which usually carries the maximum penalty. A valid declaration needs:

- At least **two sequences**.
- At least one of those must be a **pure sequence**.
- All remaining cards arranged in valid sequences or sets.

## How points are counted

In **points rummy**, the most common format, the winner scores zero and every other player counts penalty points for cards not in valid groups. Individual apps and tables can set different caps, so check the rules screen of the table you join.

- Number cards (2 to 10) count their face value.
- Jacks, Queens, Kings and Aces count 10 points each.
- Jokers count zero.
- Common caps are 80 points for a full hand, 20 for a **first drop** (leaving before your first draw) and 40 for a **middle drop** (leaving later in the hand). A wrong declaration usually costs 80.
- If you have no pure sequence, all your cards usually count, even ones in sets.


## Rummy variants: Points, Pool and Deals

- **Points rummy** is a single hand. Scores are settled at the end of each deal.
- **Pool rummy** continues over several hands. Players are eliminated when their running total reaches a limit, commonly 101 or 201 points, and the last player remaining wins.
- **Deals rummy** is played over a fixed number of deals, with chips moving from losers to winners after each one. The player with the most chips at the end wins.

## Tips for beginners

- **Build your pure sequence first.** Without it, you cannot declare, and your whole hand counts against you.
- **Discard high cards early** if they are not part of a group, to reduce penalty points if someone else declares.
- **Use jokers where they matter most**, usually to complete an impure sequence or a set, not in the pure sequence.
- **Track the open deck** to see what opponents are collecting.
- **Consider dropping early** if your starting hand is very weak; a first drop costs less than a full loss.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a pure sequence in rummy?

A pure sequence is three or more consecutive cards of the same suit with no joker, such as 3♣ 4♣ 5♣. You need at least one to make a valid declaration.

### How many sequences do you need to declare in rummy?

At least two sequences, and at least one must be pure. The rest of your cards must be in valid sequences or sets.

### Can a joker be used in a set?

Yes. A joker can replace a missing card in a set or in an impure sequence. It cannot be part of a pure sequence.

### How are points calculated in rummy?

Unarranged number cards count their face value, face cards and Aces count 10, and jokers count zero. The total is usually capped at 80 points.

### What is the difference between a first drop and a middle drop?

A first drop means leaving before your first draw and usually costs 20 points. A middle drop means leaving later in the hand and usually costs 40 points.

## Final takeaway

Rummy comes down to one rule: arrange all 13 cards into valid groups, with at least two sequences and one of them pure. Build the pure sequence first, shed high cards you cannot use, and always read the table's own rules, because apps can change caps and scoring.
